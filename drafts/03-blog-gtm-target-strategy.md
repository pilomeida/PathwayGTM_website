# [Working title] Setting Targets Before You Have Proof

## Angle
Most GTM target-setting is bottom-up — ask the regions/teams closest to the customer what they can commit to. That's usually right. This is the story of when it's wrong: a strategically critical, not-yet-available product where bottom-up planning would have anchored commitment too low and cost years of momentum. The mechanism (target brackets + a "pardon" clause for factors outside the team's control) is directly reusable by any reader setting ambitious targets under real uncertainty.

## Outline

**Hook:** Open with the standard planning assumption — ask the people closest to execution for their number, roll it up. Then the reframe: that only works if the people closest to execution actually understand what they're being asked to sell.

**H2 — When Bottom-Up Planning Quietly Undersells the Strategy**
Ground in the STAR's situation: cloud infrastructure as a board-level strategic imperative, not yet GA, no market references, regions unfamiliar with the product. Bottom-up would have produced conservative, hedged numbers — technically "safe," strategically wrong.

**H2 — The Target Bracket, Not a Fixed Number**
Explain the construct: each region gets a range, calibrated so the aggregate mid-points hit the board ambition, while regions keep a degree of ownership within the frame. This is the reusable mechanic — readers can apply "bracket, not point estimate" to their own planning.

**H2 — The Pardon Mechanism**
The fairness problem this solves: asking regions to commit to a target for a product that might not even ship on time. The explicit caveat — shortfalls attributable to product unavailability don't hit bonus calculations — converts an unfair ask into an acceptable one. Worth calling out as a governance-adjacent move (ties loosely to Governance & Steering, worth a cross-link once that WP has its own article).

**H2 — Getting the Product Line and the Field Talking**
The MoU between product line and regional practice heads as an artifact — not just a target number but a mutual-obligation document. This is what made the plan durable rather than just approved.

**H2 — Result and the Real Lesson**
€500M year-one target set, all ten regions bought in rather than overruled, a working relationship created between two groups that hadn't had one before. Land on: the target was the visible output, but the planning process itself was the actual deliverable.

**Blockquote candidate:** "If a strategic imperative doesn't come with a different planning process, what makes it strategic?" (paraphrase of the STAR's own framing — check exact wording against the STAR file before using verbatim.)
