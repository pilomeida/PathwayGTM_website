# [Working title] Jobs, Not Personas: Rethinking ICP

## Angle
Most ICP work starts with firmographics and demographics — company size, industry, title. JTBD starts somewhere else entirely: what job is the customer hiring you to do. The two external sources here (Ulwick's JTBD book, Gartner's journey-mapping report) both push against a persona-first approach to ICP, from different angles — worth citing that Pathway's own team had already independently filed both under this exact Work Package, which is a stronger endorsement than anything Claude could construct after the fact.

## Outline

**Hook:** A persona slide with a name, age, and stock photo has never closed a deal. Reframe: personas describe who; jobs-to-be-done describes why — and why is what actually predicts buying behavior.

**H2 — The Persona Trap**
Firmographic/demographic ICP definitions feel rigorous but often just describe your existing customer base, not the underlying need. Two companies with identical firmographics can have completely different jobs to be done.

**H2 — What JTBD Actually Asks**
Summarize Ulwick's core mechanic from the wiki page: customers "hire" a product/service to get a job done, and the job — not the customer profile — is the stable unit of analysis over time. Pull 1-2 concrete outcome statements from the source if the wiki page has them.

**H2 — Journey-Mapping vs. Job-Mapping: Gartner's Distinction**
Explicitly note (per the wiki page) that Gartner's own material treats these as distinct — journey maps are about the buying process, job maps are about the underlying need. Useful to readers who've conflated the two.

**H2 — Where ICP and Customer Experience Actually Meet**
This is the WP's own thesis: ICP isn't just a filtering criterion for sales — it should directly shape what "good experience" means for that segment, because different jobs imply different experience expectations.

**H2 — Applying This Inside N²**
Practical translation: how a reader would actually rewrite their ICP definition starting from jobs rather than firmographics. Keep this concrete and short — this is the "so what do I do Monday" section every long-form piece on the site seems to include implicitly.

**Blockquote candidate:** something built from the JTBD framing, e.g. "Customers don't buy products. They hire them to do a job — and if you don't know the job, you don't know your customer."
