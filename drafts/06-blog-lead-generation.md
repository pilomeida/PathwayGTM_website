# [Working title] Should We Even Pursue This?

## Angle
Most lead-gen content is about getting more leads. This is about the opposite discipline — deciding which leads deserve pursuit at all, before resource gets spent chasing ones that were never going to convert profitably. It's also the article that shifts the site from Module 1 (readiness) into Module 2 (execution) territory for the first time in this batch — worth a short bridging line acknowledging the shift ("everything so far has been about getting ready to sell — this is where Nerve actually starts").

## Outline

**Hook:** "How do we win this deal?" is the wrong first question. The right one is "should we be pursuing it at all — and what would winning actually cost us?"

**H2 — Chasing Everything Is Its Own Failure Mode**
Ground in the STAR's situation: a commercial team engaging too many complex, multi-year opportunities without a structured qualification model, converting at low margins or not at all, disproportionate pre-sales effort with no filter.

**H2 — The Lead Business Profile**
Walk through the framework's actual fields: project scope/scale, multi-year revenue potential, decision-making structure, power-base mapping, competitor positioning, timeline, deal strategy, risk and mitigation. The "power base mapping" element is worth calling out specifically — it's the piece that signals real commercial sophistication beyond a generic qualification checklist.

**H2 — The Question That Changes**
The reframe this WP needs: "let's win this" becomes "should we pursue this — and what would it take to win profitably?" This is the section that does the most work — it's a one-sentence mental model shift a reader can apply immediately.

**H2 — Embedding It Through Use, Not Email**
How the framework actually got adopted: hands-on working sessions on live pipeline opportunities, not a template rollout. A craft point about change management, not just framework design — readers who've watched a good framework die in an inbox will recognize this.

**H2 — Portable Across Sectors**
Brief note that the same logic had already been applied at Ericsson during a struggling P&L period with equivalent results — evidence this isn't industry-specific.

**H2 — Result**
Win rate and margin improved; the team gained a shared language for pursuit decisions. Land on: qualification discipline is a commercial-strategy tool, not an admin process.

**Blockquote candidate:** "The question isn't how do we win this. It's should we pursue this at all — and what would winning actually cost us?"
